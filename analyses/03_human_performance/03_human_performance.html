Human performance metrics

Date: 29/06/2025

GOHREP

Goal:

Calculate metrics of human scoring agreement to be used as model benchmarks.

Hypothesis:

Human scorers will not always agree.

Rationale:

Scoring is subjective, particularly given the scoring key’s simple nature.

Experimental plan: Calculate two metrics across 1,000 class-normalized samples of the scoring data. Plot their distributions and calculate means, confidence intervals, and standard deviations. - Metric 1: Percentage agreement of each individual score to median score of same CDA. - Metric 2: Percentage agreement of individual scores to each other.

# 3.1
def get_dist_stats(dist, name):
    mean_agreement = np.mean(dist)
    std_agreement = np.std(dist)
    sem_agreement = st.sem(dist)
    ci_agreement = st.t.interval(confidence=0.95, df=len(dist)-1, loc=mean_agreement, scale=sem_agreement)
    ci_lower, ci_upper = ci_agreement
    ci_diff = (ci_upper - ci_lower) / 2
    print(name)
    print(f"Mean: {mean_agreement:.4f}")
    print(f"95% CI: {ci_diff}, ({ci_agreement[0]:.5f}, {ci_agreement[1]:.5f})")
    print(f"SD: {std_agreement:.4f}\n")

5. Leave-one-out variant of Metric 1

Metric 1 compares each individual score to the CDA median — but that median includes the score being evaluated, so a scorer automatically matches whenever they were the middle of the three (the median of an odd set is one of its members). This inflates Metric 1. The leave-one-out (LOO) variant removes the confound: each score is compared to the median of the other two scorers on that CDA. With two remaining scorers the LOO reference is their mean, which is a half-integer whenever they disagree by an odd amount — so no integer score can match it exactly. LOO exact agreement is therefore necessarily stricter than Metric 1, and the near-miss (within ±1) figure is the more directly comparable one.

Computed the same way as Metric 1: across 1,000 class-normalised downsamples, reporting the distribution of exact and near-miss agreement. Results are cached to loo_metric1_results.csv.

Code 
# 5
loo_results_path = repo_root / "analyses" / "03_human_performance" / "loo_metric1_results.csv"

def loo_metric1_accuracy(df_wide, precision):
    """Agreement of each score with the leave-one-out median (mean) of the other two scorers."""
    s = df_wide[["Score1", "Score2", "Score3"]].to_numpy(dtype=float)
    diffs = []
    for k in range(3):
        others = np.delete(s, k, axis=1)        # the other two scorers
        ref = np.nanmean(others, axis=1)        # median of two = their mean
        diffs.append(np.abs(s[:, k] - ref))
    d = np.concatenate(diffs)
    d = d[~np.isnan(d)]
    return float(np.mean(d <= precision))

6. Inter-rater vs intra-rater agreement (non-zero CDAs)

For the five scorers who completed the rescore task, we can directly compare how consistently each scorer agrees with themselves (intra-rater, from 06_intra_rater_reliability) versus how consistently they agree with other scorers on the same CDAs (inter-rater, computed here from the canonical data). Both metrics are restricted to non-zero median CDAs, since zero-score CDAs are near-unanimously agreed upon and would inflate both figures.

If intra-rater > inter-rater: individual biases are a genuine additional source of disagreement on top of task ambiguity. If intra-rater ≈ inter-rater: the task is inherently ambiguous and even self-consistency is low.

What this shows:

  • For most scorers, intra-rater consistency exceeds inter-rater agreement, meaning each individual is more consistent with themselves than with others. This confirms that systematic individual biases contribute to inter-rater disagreement on top of genuine task ambiguity.
  • The mean intra-rater reference line sets a natural ceiling for inter-rater agreement: different scorers cannot reliably agree more than a single scorer agrees with themselves.
