Intra-Rater Reliability

Date: 2026-06-05

GOHREP

Goal:

Quantify how consistently each scorer scores the same CDA on two separate occasions (intra-rater reliability). Assess whether rescoring changes agreement with the original ground truth (median score). Investigate how rescoring disagreements would change ground truth labels (median score).

Hypothesis:

  1. Scorers will be more consistent with themselves on extreme scores (1, 6) and less consistent on intermediate scores (2–5).
  2. Rescoring will not systematically improve or worsen agreement with the median — any changes will reflect genuine scoring uncertainty rather than learning.

Rationale:

Only non-zero median CDAs were included in the rescoring set, since 0s are almost always agreed upon. Each scorer was presented the same CDAs they originally scored, in a randomised order, without access to their original scores.

Experimental plan:

  1. Load and anonymise all rescore CSVs.
  2. Intra-rater reliability: compare old_score vs new_score per scorer, including self-agreement by original score class.
  3. Score change distribution: characterise the direction and magnitude of score changes.
  4. Ground truth labels: assess how often rescoring disagreements would change the median label.
  5. Within- vs between-scorer variation: compare intra-rater disagreement (this analysis) with inter-rater disagreement (analysis 04).

7. Within- vs between-scorer variation

This section compares two kinds of disagreement on the same set of non-zero-median CDAs:

  1. Within-scorer: For each rescored CDA, what is the difference between the new and old score.
  2. Between-scorer: For each CDA, what is the difference between each pair of original scorers (Scorer1 - Scorer2, Scorer1 - Scorer3, Scorer2 - Scorer3). Pairwise scorer-vs-scorer comparison, not a comparison to the median.

Both are restricted to non-zero-median CDAs.

If the within- and between-scorer spreads are similar, most label noise is irreducible scoring uncertainty rather than systematic between-scorer bias.

The same ordinal Krippendorff’s α used for the inter-rater panel in analysis 04, applied here to each scorer’s two occasions (treating old_score and new_score as two raters). Using one coefficient for both keeps intra- and inter-rater reliability on a single comparable scale, and the ordinal metric respects that a 1↔︎6 disagreement is worse than 1↔︎2 (the reason α was chosen over % agreement). It also handles the missing values natively. Per-scorer 95% CIs are bootstrapped over CDAs; the dashed line is the panel inter-rater α on the same non-zero-median CDAs.

What this shows:

  • Running Krippendorf’s alpha (measure of agreement between two scorers) treating the original and new scores as different scorers.
  • Each red point is a scorer’s within-rater krippendorf alpha value with its 95% confidence interval. The dashed line is the inter-rater krippendorff alpha.
  • Points above the line mean scorers are more consistent with themselves than with others.
  • Most scorers are on or above the line, meaning they agree with themselves more than the average scorer pair agrees with each other.
  • Scorer 9 agrees with themself less consistently.
